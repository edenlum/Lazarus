# The young family in World War I

[[Arthur Lazarus]] and [[Käte Lazarus]] had been married only **eleven months** when war began in 1914, as Arthur recalls in his memoir (PDF pp.6, 8).

Their first son [[Walter Lazarus]] was born on **22 September 1914**. In January 1915 the family learned that Käte's brother [[Hans Wolff]], then 20, had been killed in military service (memoir pp.8–9).

Arthur was mobilized into the artillery in **April 1915**. He writes that Käte remained in their Berlin apartment with a seven-month-old baby, while he spent much of the next four years elsewhere, with occasional leave. He was stationed mostly in Karlsruhe and says his own wartime assignment was comparatively safe and agreeable, though he describes the family separation (memoir p.9).

Kurt was born in 1917; a photograph in the memoir shows the two boys with Käte in **1918** (memoir PDF p.12, text transcription not yet visually reviewed).

The documents reveal two different kinds of wartime experience within one family: the soldier who later remembers tolerable duties, and a young mother looking after children while separated from her husband. We have **Arthur's** account here, not Käte's independent WWI diary.

**Source:** Arthur memoir, PDF pp.6, 8–9 (verified); p.12 (photograph/text pending review).
