# Two family homes

Two homes appear repeatedly in the documents, separated by geography, war and time.

## Dahlem, Berlin

[[Arthur Lazarus]] and [[Käte Lazarus]] built a house in [[Dahlem]] and moved there in December **1922**. Arthur recalls the garden, children's swimming pool and fruit trees affectionately. A photograph is captioned **summer 1925** (memoir PDF pp.14–15).

## Shaar Hefer

After living in Jerusalem during the 1948 siege, the family moved to [[Shaar Hefer]]. Arthur writes that their sons encouraged the move and describes a bungalow. But he says his eyesight and other age-related ailments made rural life difficult and that Käte never fully adjusted (memoir PDF p.18).

The **same-looking house photograph** occurs in the memoir (PDF p.18, captioned **1950**) and in the 1948 letters compilation (PDF p.35, captioned **1951**). The date cannot be resolved from these two captions alone; see [[Photographs and caption conflicts]].

## What a story could explore

A home built during success in Germany, abandoned after persecution, followed much later by a house built after another war: a striking structure. But the sources tell us **Arthur's feelings** about each place and only a little about Käte's. An emotional retelling should keep that asymmetry rather than invent her inner life.

**Sources:** memoir pp.14–15, 17–18; letters compilation p.35.
