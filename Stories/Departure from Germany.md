# Departure from Germany

This was a **three-year process**, not a single event. [[Arthur Lazarus]] says he understood in April **1933** that his children had no future in Germany, but explicitly dates the family’s eventual departure to **November 1936** (memoir PDF pp.16–17).

## What happened to the children?

[[Walter Lazarus]], who had just passed his Abitur and expected to start a Reichsbahn apprenticeship, was instead trained in mechanical engineering in Chemnitz and Winterthur. [[Kurt Lazarus]] entered agricultural training (*Hachsharah*) on a farm in Holland. [[Brigitte Lazarus]] moved to a Zionist school in Berlin. Arthur says the sons joined Zionist youth movements (memoir p.17).

## September 1933: an exploratory journey

Arthur and [[Käte Lazarus]] sailed on the **SS Roma** to Palestine, unsure it was right for them. Arthur remembers the heat, unfamiliar countryside and feeling that things were unfinished. He also saw its distance from Nazi Germany as a compelling reason to consider settling there (memoir p.17).

## November 1936: leaving home

Arthur’s date for their departure is **November 1936**. They then settled in [[Tel Aviv]], eventually moving to [[Jerusalem]] around 1941 (memoir p.17).

The personal cost becomes more tangible alongside [[Life in Dahlem]]: the comfortable family house, orchard, children's pool and vacations Arthur remembered so fondly.

**Source:** `Scans/Arthur-Lazarus-Memoir.pdf`, PDF pp.14–17. These recollections were written in 1953 and the surviving English document was translated in 2006. Individual family members may have moved on different dates: do not assume all emigrated together.
