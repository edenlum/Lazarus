# Letters that could not be sent

*May–June 1948, Jerusalem — a source-based reading of [[Käte Lazarus]]'s letters, not a fictional reconstruction.*

On **1 May 1948**, Käte begins by noticing the spring. Flowers seem especially bright because, during the siege, animals have not been grazing them. The city is isolated; a reply written by "Dad" is still lying there, unable to go through the post. She turns quickly from the beautiful day to the thing that hurts most: **no contact with her children and grandchildren** (letters PDF p.2).

The letter speaks of mere survival and of trying to hold onto hope. Arthur was evidently in Jerusalem with her: Käte writes of "אבא" answering correspondence and going about his Saturday visits. This strongly suggests the letters went **to loved ones outside the city, not to Arthur**, though the recipients have not yet been identified conclusively.

On **5 May**, a letter that had taken **18 days** to reach Jerusalem finally arrives. Käte describes crying as she reads words from loved ones, though she cannot send out the letter she began on 1 May (letters PDF p.7, opening paragraph visually checked).

On **15 June**, Käte writes that she has finally heard, by telegram, that relatives at Shaar Hefer are well. She feels she has not suffered hunger and fear in vain if the children and grandchildren are safe. But relief does not end longing. Now she wants more news and to see them (letters PDF p.31).

By the **23 June** letter, tiny improvements carry weight: fish, vegetables, an orange, talk of restoring electricity and water, and even a trip to the cinema. She hopes earlier letters reached their recipients. Then she asks that her diary be kept. Her closing wish is that **such bitter times never again interrupt the correspondence between them** (letters PDF p.35).

This is the emotional arc visible in the verified pages: **isolation → uncertain hope → family news → a wish to preserve the connection**. It is an interpretation of the surviving material, not an invented scene or dialogue.
