A story from the archive / No. 01

The letters that
could not be sent

One woman's correspondence from a city under siege — and what she worried about most.

1948

War can be told through battles and borders. Käte's letters tell it through something smaller: the unbearable wait for news from people she loves.

1 May
Saturday · Jerusalem
I · The silence

The city is full of flowers. The post is empty.

Spring arrives strangely beautiful. Without the animals that once grazed near Jerusalem, Käte notices flowers in unusually vivid colours. But the city has been cut off. Mail can no longer leave. A reply written by her husband is still there, waiting for a road that will open.

Far away are the children and grandchildren. She cannot know how they are. What begins as a description of the weather becomes a letter about missing the people who give her life meaning.

Based on Käte's 1 May letter · Hebrew translated compilation · PDF p. 2
5 May
A letter finally arrives
II · Eighteen days

A letter travels through the silence.

Käte is still unable to send the letter she wrote on 1 May. But another letter has somehow reached her — written by family on 16 April, eighteen days earlier. It has been in the country for some time, though planes have stopped arriving in Jerusalem.

She is overwhelmed by the words she reads. The letter does not end the war or reopen the roads. For a few moments, it lets people she loves reach her across the distance.

Based on the visually checked opening of Käte's 5 May letter · Hebrew translated compilation · PDF p. 7
15 June
A message arrives
III · The news

At last, a telegram.

Weeks later, a message reaches Jerusalem: relatives in Shaar Hefer are well. For Käte, the news is almost more precious than food. She writes of the relief of knowing her family is alive and safe.

And then, just as naturally, the next longing appears. Knowing they are safe is not the same as seeing them. She wants another letter, another detail, the possibility of being together again.

Based on Käte's 15 June letter · Hebrew translated compilation · PDF p. 31
23 June
The closing pages
IV · Ordinary life

Fish. Vegetables. An orange.

The things she writes about are almost disarmingly ordinary. Supplies are beginning to return: fish, vegetables, even an orange for each person. There is talk of electricity and water. She has been to the cinema again, after months without it.

She hopes earlier letters reached their destinations. Before ending the collection, she asks that her diary and correspondence be kept. It is a request that the family, generations later, has made possible.

Based on Käte's 23 June letter · Hebrew translated compilation · PDF p. 35

She was not trying to write history. She was trying to reach her family. History is what survived.

Historical note. This is narrative nonfiction, not a quotation or a recreation of an original German letter. Käte's surviving text here is a Hebrew translation published in a family compilation in 2006. The illustrations are decorative; no invented words have been attributed to her.

How to read this archive

Remember the distance between an event and its retelling.

  • Käte's text on this site is paraphrased from a Hebrew translation of German letters.
  • Arthur's memoir is retrospective, written years after some events.
  • Photographs and captions were added later and sometimes disagree.
  • No scene, dialogue or facial likeness on this page is presented as an authentic recovered recording.
